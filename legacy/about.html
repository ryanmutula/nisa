Nisa Perfumes buys Arabic attars, niche houses and international designer fragrance from the houses themselves and their appointed distributors, and moves it into five East and Southern African markets. We sell two ways from the same holding: direct to you, one bottle at a time, and wholesale to the trade — boutiques, salons, pharmacies, duty-free and online resellers. The whole business is arranged around one problem: a perfume that travels badly does not smell like the perfume that was signed off.

The chain

Four steps, no detours.

Fewer handovers is the single biggest thing you can do for a fragile product.

Bought at source

Direct from each house or its appointed distributor. No grey market, no parallel imports, no bottles of unknown age. Every carton arrives with paperwork that ties it to a batch.

Consolidated once

Everything lands at one Nairobi point and is checked against the packing list before it moves again. One handover instead of four is the single biggest thing you can do for a fragile product.

Kept out of the heat

Fragrance oxidises in a hot warehouse and light flattens the top notes. Ours is shaded, ventilated and stocked away from direct sun; fast-moving attars sit on the coolest wall.

Shipped to the region

Nairobi next day. Kampala, Kigali and Dar es Salaam within the week. Lusaka on a weekly consolidated run, which keeps the freight cost off the bottle price.

What we will and will not do

Authenticity is a supply chain, not a sticker.

Anyone can print a certificate. What actually keeps a counterfeit out of your hands is refusing to buy from anyone who cannot say where a bottle came from — and being willing to lose a margin over it.
